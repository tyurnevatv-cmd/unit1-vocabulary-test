11 · Beginner grammarDrozdova · English Grammar

THERE IS / THERE ARE

Study the grammar first. Then apply it to military vocabulary.

CADET · A1–A2

Introduce something new

THERE + BE + NOUN (+ PLACE / TIME)

There is a checkpoint near the headquarters.
There are two officers at the briefing.
There is some equipment in the vehicle.

Singular / uncountable: there is · there was
Plural: there are · there were
Future: there will be

Ask and make negative statements

Is there a briefing today?
Are there any new orders?
There isn't a vehicle outside.
There aren't any officers here.

Compare: There is a briefing in the hall (new information) vs The briefing is in the hall (its location is already known).

Reference: Drozdova et al., English Grammar, § 2, pp. 11–13.

13 · Advanced grammarAdvanced Grammar in Use · Units 95 & 97

BEYOND THERE IS / ARE

C1–C2: existence, information structure and grammatical choices.

1. Agreement in lists

There was a briefing, two interpreters and an officer.
There were two interpreters, a briefing and an officer.

In formal edited English, agreement normally follows the first item. Informal there's + plural occurs, but avoid it in a translation test.

2. Modals & reporting

There may be a delay.
There seems to be a misunderstanding.
There arose a disagreement.

The existential construction is not limited to is/are.

3. There being...

There being no new orders, the unit remained in position.

Equivalent in meaning to Because there were no new orders...; compact and formal.

4. It or there?

It is essential to verify the intelligence.
There is no point in repeating the same error.
It's no secret that the briefing was cancelled.

Compare introductory it and existential there; some expressions are fixed.

References: Hewings, Advanced Grammar in Use, 4th ed., Unit 95 (pp. 190–191), Unit 97 (pp. 194–195). Military examples adapted.
